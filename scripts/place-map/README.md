# Place-map source boundary

`generate.py` is the offline source boundary. It verifies the exact source manifest, archive bytes, required shapefile sidecars, and GMT server manifest. `generate.mjs` is the only pack producer: it consumes that verified cache, decodes the pinned shapefiles and the two GMT relief grids, simplifies and quantises the geometry, and writes the checked-in binary pack. Decoding the locator-tier relief grid shells out to the `opj_decompress` CLI from the openjpeg project (for example `brew install openjpeg` or `apt install openjpeg-tools`), the same way the source verifier already requires `python3`.

Put the pinned source objects and `earth_relief_server.txt` in a separate cache, then run `MOSS_PLACE_MAP_SOURCE_DIR=/path/to/cache python3 generate.py --check` followed by `MOSS_PLACE_MAP_SOURCE_DIR=/path/to/cache pnpm generate`. The cache is intentionally outside the repository; a missing object or hash mismatch is an error and never triggers a network fallback. The generator writes `crates/moss-build/data/place-map/place-map-v1.bin.br` and its machine-readable size report. That file is the pack brotli-compressed at quality 11, not the raw `MOSSPLM1` bytes: it is what `place_map::embedded()` embeds verbatim via `include_bytes!` and decompresses once at runtime, so it is also what the budget below measures. The checked-in pack is capped at 3,513,889 bytes (about 3.35 MiB) of brotli-compressed bytes in that `.br` file, with a 5% margin enforced by the generator and by `scripts/place-map/test_generate.mjs`. The generator checks the budget before writing, so a run over it fails without replacing the checked-in pack or its report; `place-map-size.json`'s `raw_bytes` is the pre-compression size, kept for per-layer diagnostics only, and `brotli_bytes` is the gated figure. The budget only ever moves down from here: it came down by exactly what the world tier's bands and minor layers freed when they were dropped, less what the coast-following shelf band and area-simplified rivers spent. It sits above the pack's own measured size (about 3.33 MB) rather than at it, because the biggest lever still on the table — storing each relief and sea-floor band's shared boundary once instead of once per band — is estimated to save roughly another 0.4 MB and hasn't been built yet.

Schema 4 appends a fourth, length-delimited section after the tile index: named cities, mountain ranges, peaks and rivers for the places-explorer's map labels, in `en` and `zh-Hant` (`encodeLabels`/`place_map::labels::decode`). This is data only — nothing in this repository yet places these labels on a rendered map. Natural Earth's own `scalerank` picks which features are "major" enough to include (see the cutoff constants next to `encodeLabels` for the reasoning and the current counts); mountain ranges are a Natural Earth polygon dataset, so each range's label point is its largest ring's area-weighted centroid rather than an authored point. A river's label line is the SAME area-simplified geometry its pack layer already encodes, read a second time rather than referenced by id — the pack carries no stable per-feature id a label could point at instead. Measured at generation time, the label payload adds about 78 KiB raw (522 cities, 222 ranges, 269 peaks, 495 rivers) and about 48 KiB once brotli-compressed together with the rest of the pack, leaving the combined pack at 3,328,141 brotli bytes — a 5.29% budget margin, still over the 5% floor `writePack` enforces. A future label-set change that pushes the margin under 5% must move to a sibling `labels.bin` file with its own budget instead of growing this one past its floor.

The pack is `MOSSPLM1`, schema 4. Its fixed header contains the quantisation factor, WGS84 extent, tier/layer counts, 10-degree tile size, the SHA-256 of this source manifest, and ten stable layer-to-source bit masks (opaque to the decoder; a mask names which logical source datasets feed a layer, not a literal per-file index). Each tier then contains length-delimited layer records; each feature carries an integer bounding box, a signed 16-bit band, and delta/zig-zag varints for its parts. The band is a relief or sea-floor feature's threshold, and a river's Natural Earth `scalerank` (0 for the largest rivers), which the renderer uses to taper river strokes; every other layer stores 0. The final record is a global 10-degree tile index into the fine-tier feature sequence. Tier 0 holds land alone, simplified by about a degree, for the globe inset; tier 1 holds every stored layer, and every main map, the world map included, draws from it. Layer IDs are stable: coast 1, land 2, lakes 3, rivers 4, ice 5, reefs 6, salt flats 7, built-up 8, relief 9, sea floor 10 — but the pack never stores a coast record: Natural Earth's coastline and land datasets trace the same digitized shoreline, so the renderer draws every coast from land's own rings instead of storing both independently: a map's coast is a soft halo of its land paths, and the globe inset strokes its land rings. Relief thresholds are `100, 200, 400, 700, 1000, 1500, 2000, 2500, 3000, 4000, 5000, 6000`. Sea-floor thresholds are `-6000, -5000, -4000, -3000, -2000, -1000, -200, -100, -50, -30, -20, -10`, including three shallow shelf-sea steps so shelf seas render distinctly instead of flattening to one deep-water tone. A relief band is the ground at or above its threshold; a sea-floor band is the water at or below its threshold, that is at least that deep, traced from the negated grid. The shallowest sea-floor band also takes in every grid sample off Natural Earth land, so it runs to the coastline the land layer draws: traced from the grid alone, it stopped where the grid's coarser coast runs shallow, and around island chains the coast halo drawn under the sea floor showed as a wide bright band. Both ladders therefore nest and shrink away from sea level, which is the order the renderer paints them in. Both ladders are data carried in feature bands; the decoder does not prescribe their count or values. The thresholds come from pinned `d3-contour` isobands traced from the 3-arcminute GMT grid at its native resolution, so relief and sea-floor bands hold their shape down at locator scale. Band rings are simplified by area (Visvalingam-Whyatt at the tolerance squared) rather than by distance from a chord, which would collapse an edge meandering across a flat plain into one long straight line. Rivers are simplified by area too, as open lines whose ends stay put, for the same reason: a river winding within a pixel of its course lost every bend. Every other layer keeps Douglas-Peucker. Coordinates are quantised to a thousandth of a degree. The deepest Natural Earth bathymetry band is retained as a coastline-safe floor. Unknown schemas and layer IDs are rejected by the Rust decoder.

Fine-tier simplification also splits by role: land (the sole source of the coast stroke) is Douglas-Peucker simplified at 1 device pixel, every other layer (lakes, rivers, ice, reefs, salt flats, built-up, relief, and sea-floor bands, the bands and rivers by area at that tolerance squared) at 2 device pixels, both measured at the roughly 10-degree-wide, 720px-wide locator frame. Because coast is now always land's own ring boundary, the two can no longer disagree at render scale the way two independently-simplified datasets could.

Both ladders are in the grid's stored units, not metres. The GMT server stores `earth_relief` as 16-bit integers with a scale factor of 0.5 (the server manifest lists 0.5 for the 3-arcminute tiles), and the generator reads the stored integers without applying it. A stored unit is therefore half a metre: the `100` band traces ground about 50 m up and the `-6000` band traces water about 3,000 m deep. This is deliberate. The approved basemap design was drawn from the same unscaled values, so applying the scale without also halving both ladders would change its look; doing both is a design change, not a fix.

The source manifest records the Natural Earth layer release metadata, exact ZIP members, SHA-256 values, and public-domain notice. The GMT server manifest is pinned by date and SHA-256; because that upstream manifest does not publish a separate licence statement, `NOTICE` preserves that limitation instead of asserting one.
